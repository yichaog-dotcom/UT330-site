October 7 research lab: coffee shop hours

October 12, 2026 · Reflection on the October 7 lab

In the October 7 design research lab, our group chose the coffee shop case. The shop closes in the late afternoon, and we wanted to understand what determines its hours and what would make evening opening worthwhile. We wrote interview questions and used AI-simulated participants, including a manager and a barista, to practice interviewing.

The useful part for me was matching the questions to the person. A manager can talk about staffing and sales, while a barista can describe what happens during a shift. Asking someone to walk through closing brought out more detail than simply asking whether the shop should stay open later.

In the simulated responses, the manager focused on uneven evening demand, labor costs, and having enough people available to work. The barista described cleaning machines, restocking, and serving customers while trying to close. Comparing the two perspectives made it easier to see how staying open later could also mean finishing work much later.

One point that stayed with me was that a shop can look busy without making enough money to cover an extra shift. Someone studying for several hours may only buy one drink. I would organize our notes around customer demand, staffing, costs, and closing tasks, then use those themes to decide what to ask next.

These were simulated interviews, so they gave us practice rather than evidence that a real shop should change its hours. Before recommending anything, I would want to speak with actual staff and compare evening sales with staffing costs. The lab was a useful reminder to understand how the business works before jumping into a design solution.
